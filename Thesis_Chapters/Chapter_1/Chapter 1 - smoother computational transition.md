# Chapter 1: a lighter transition into computational analysis

These three proposed edits use the updated `Thesis Chapter 1.pdf`. Page numbers refer to that PDF. They introduce computation briefly through the chapter's existing concern with relationships, dispersed evidence and historical reconstruction. They do not add a methodology section or assume the previously suggested literature has been incorporated.

## 1. Section 1.1, PDF page 2: an early signpost

Append these two sentences to the paragraph ending “the economy of salt production, exchange, and trade in the Himalayas”, immediately before the chapter outline:

> This emphasis on social relationships also raises the question of how connections between communities, institutions and places can be traced across historical sources. It informs the thesis’s use of computational analysis to examine these connections, guided by the historical and anthropological perspectives reviewed here.

This gives the reader early notice of computation and connects its purpose to the social science discussion. The accounts of Mauss, Polanyi, Scott, Thompson and Parthasarathi can continue as they stand.

## 2. Section 1.4, PDF page 9: connect the research gap to its analytical purpose

At the end of the final paragraph, after “state authorities, frontier markets, and other commodities”, add:

> Bringing these scattered accounts together would allow the relationships described in individual sources to be examined as part of a wider pattern of exchange. Computational analysis can assist this reconstruction, while historical interpretation remains essential to understanding the obligations, institutions and inequalities underlying those relationships.

This explains what computation contributes immediately after the problem that motivates it. It introduces no tools, algorithms or technical procedures.

## 3. Section 1.5, PDF page 10: make the conclusion recall that purpose

Replace only the sentence beginning “Computational analysis supplements conventional historical analysis…” and its citation with:

> Computational analysis supports this task by helping to trace connections across dispersed archival accounts (Painter et al., 2019). The social and historical perspectives reviewed in this chapter guide the interpretation of the resulting patterns of exchange.

Keep the following paragraph about the next chapter's archival sources. Its closing reference to building historical networks now follows an approach already introduced to the reader.

## Editorial scope

The progression becomes: exchange is socially embedded → its relationships need to be traced across sources → computation helps examine those connections → social and historical interpretation gives them meaning.

These are proposed wording changes. The original chapter files are unchanged. The existing Painter citation is retained, not independently verified; the edits make no claim that the social theorists themselves advocated computational methods.
