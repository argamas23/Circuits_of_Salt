## Two additional checks against local primary sources

**Bashahr:** `Books/1910.pdf`, PDF p. 100, printed p. **63**, directly describes cash and barter purchase, contrasting exchange ratios, profit, and distinct salt/borax/wool/pashm conditions. This page was checked against its scan, not only OCR. The title page labels the volume *Punjab States Gazetteer*, VIII, *Simla Hill States*, 1910, but the Lahore imprint is **1911**. Cite the checked edition as Punjab Government (1911 [volume designated 1910]). This is administrative evidence for Chapter 2 and a check on Chapter 1’s assumptions; it is not a substitute for scholarly comparison.

**Kulu and Saraj:** `Books/Kangra Gazatteer.pdf`, PDF p. 143, printed p. **129**, checked visually. The account explains how moving the registration post to Kyelang and changing classifications affected recorded trade. It also distinguishes wool’s value, cash purchases, Tibetan duties, salt, and animal-borne transport. Accordingly, official trade figures have institutional histories; a graph assembled from their prose cannot be treated as a direct census of the economy. The original page contains colonial labels for communities that should be contextualised when discussed.

## What this changes in the chapter

The proposed comparison is an interpretative synthesis: ecological differences explain why exchange was useful; institutions explain participation and distribution; political history explains changing jurisdiction and access; infrastructure and provisioning policies explain uneven reorganisation. None of these strands alone establishes salt as the region’s universal economic backbone.
