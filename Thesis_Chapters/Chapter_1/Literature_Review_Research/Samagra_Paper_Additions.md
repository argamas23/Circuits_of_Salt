## Material not carried forward as established evidence

- The **anticipated results** on p. 4 and retrospective-sounding conclusions on pp. 4–5 do not demonstrate measured centrality, a network backbone, bottlenecks, or lexical change. These remain propositions requiring results and historical interpretation.
- The paper’s claim that *chu lag* members belong to the same *pha spun* conflicts with Ahmed’s explicit qualification on p. 42. The review retains the corrected account.
- The paper’s opposition between reciprocity and profit, and complete replacement by contract or bureaucracy, is too broad for the checked scholarship. Existing qualified comparisons remain.
- The Tingmosgang discussion describes wool/trading privileges; it does not establish a salt-specific clause. Salt taxation, licensing, and chronology need jurisdiction-specific evidence.
- The broad claim that Tibetan lakes continued supplying Ladakh after border restrictions is not transferred without corridor-specific evidence. Availability at a production site does not establish cross-border access.
- Numerous reference details require correction. The new review retains checked editions and publication details rather than copying the PDF’s bibliography: Ahmed 1999, Lecoq 2022, Bergmann 2016, Alam 2007, Ross 1982–1983, Lazcano in *Revue d’Études Tibétaines*, and the already resolved Wary/Rizvi entries.

The draft’s new paragraphs cite the underlying scholarship for historical and ethnographic claims. The self-citation identifies the project’s earlier framing and supplies no independent historical corroboration. The original PDF and the cited Chapter 1 were not edited.
