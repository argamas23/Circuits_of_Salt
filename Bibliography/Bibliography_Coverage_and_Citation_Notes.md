## Content issues left intact

These issues are recorded here because the current task authorises citations and bibliography, not a substantive rewrite. A nearby citation must not be read as validating every statement in its paragraph.

- Section 1.1: “floral extraction” is retained. The definition of netsang as “the trade of salt” conflicts with Section 1.4; no citation was attached to endorse that gloss.

- Section 1.2: “hau or mana” and the strict exclusion of contract/monetary calculation need more precise theoretical treatment. Chapter references identify the relevant work without certifying those formulations.

- Section 1.3 opening: the claim that Polanyi paid relatively little attention to transition needs reconsideration against The Great Transformation. No citation was added to endorse that claim.

- Scott: the sentence excluding abstract ideological commitments is broader than what the checked source descriptions establish.

- Parthasarathi: the 2001 introduction was checked, but the exact detailed passages for the 2011 global-divergence interpretation remain to be read; a general book reference does not establish every universal claim about market formation.

- Section 1.4: no source checked here establishes that every ecological region was unable to remain economically self-sufficient. Rizvi’s separate self-reliant-economy article needs explicit engagement before that broad claim is finalised.

- The salt-backbone assertion remains the researcher’s proposition. Citations are placed on the preceding commodity-circulation sentence, not on the assertion as an established result.

- Netsang as a universal regional system of fictive kinship, its geographical application, and the exact spelling remain unverified. Nepalese ritualised trading ties should not be assigned that term without evidence.

- Ahmed directly supports seasonal collection, chu lag/chu dpon, kotwal, allocation and conflict. The chapter’s goba role and categorical contrast with individual property rights require additional source checking.

- The general monastery paragraph asserts tax collection, tribute and redistribution. Ahmed p. 45 documents religious contributions, but that alone does not establish this whole institutional description; no blanket citation was added.

- Tso Kar chronology remains unresolved before the 1960s in Ahmed’s account. Later ethnography cannot establish unchanged precolonial arrangements. Dollfus’s post-1962 discussion was therefore not cited as evidence of a British colonial mechanism.

- Broad claims that previous Himalayan literature is merely descriptive, or never treats salt only as exchange value, require a fuller historiographical assessment.

- Chapter 2 still promises Foreign Department records; their original files have not been located. The text was retained as requested.
